Dr. APJ Abdul Kalam

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was an Indian scientist, educator, and the 11th President of India. He is fondly remembered as the "Missile Man of India" due to his significant contributions to India's space and missile programs. Kalam’s life and work have inspired millions, and his vision for a developed India continues to resonate even after his passing.

Early Life and Education

  • Born: October 15, 1931, in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, India.
  • Family: He was born to a middle-class Muslim family. His father, Jainulabdeen, was a boat owner and imam of the local mosque. His mother, Ashiamma, was a homemaker.
  • Early Interests: As a child, Kalam was interested in flying and the mysteries of the skies. He was deeply influenced by his father’s wisdom and his teacher’s encouragement.
  • Education: Kalam attended the Schwartz Higher Secondary School in Ramanathapuram and later studied at the St. Joseph’s College in Tiruchirappalli. He earned his degree in physics from the University of Madras in 1954. Subsequently, he joined the Madras Institute of Technology (MIT) where he specialized in aeronautical engineering.

Career in Science and Technology

  • Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO): Kalam started his career as a scientist at ISRO in 1969. He played a pivotal role in the development of India’s first satellite launch vehicle (SLV) and the successful launch of the Rohini satellite into orbit in 1980.
  • Missile Development: In the 1980s and 1990s, Kalam led efforts to develop indigenous missiles. He was instrumental in the development of the Prithvi and Agni missiles, which significantly strengthened India's defense capabilities. Due to his significant contributions, he earned the title of the "Missile Man of India."
  • Nuclear Tests: Under Kalam’s leadership, India conducted nuclear tests in 1998, codenamed Operation Shakti, at Pokhran in Rajasthan. These tests established India as a nuclear power.

Role as President of India

  • Elected President: Dr. Kalam was elected as the 11th President of India in 2002, serving one term from 2002 to 2007. He was known as the "People's President" due to his humility, approachability, and strong rapport with the public, especially students.
  • Vision for India: During his presidency, Kalam emphasized the development of India into a knowledge society and a global leader in technology and innovation. His vision for India's future, outlined in his book India 2020, aimed to transform India into a developed nation by the year 2020.

Post-Presidency Work

  • Teaching: After completing his tenure as President, Kalam continued to be involved in education and public service. He taught at various institutions, including the Indian Institute of Management (IIM) Ahmedabad and the Indian Institute of Management (IIM) Indore.
  • Books: Kalam authored several books, the most famous being "Wings of Fire", an autobiography detailing his early life and scientific journey, and "India 2020", where he discussed his vision for India’s future.

Legacy

  • Awards and Honors:
    • Bharat Ratna (1997), India’s highest civilian award, for his contributions to the development of India's space and defense technologies.
    • Padma Bhushan (1981) and Padma Vibhushan (1990), two other prestigious awards for his contributions to science and engineering.
  • Inspiration: Dr. Kalam's teachings, simple lifestyle, and love for science made him an enduring symbol of excellence, integrity, and hard work. He was widely respected across political and social boundaries.

Death

  • Passing: On July 27, 2015, while delivering a lecture at the Indian Institute of Management Shillong, Dr. Kalam suffered a cardiac arrest and passed away at the age of 83.

Conclusion

Dr. APJ Abdul Kalam remains one of India’s most respected and beloved figures, remembered not just for his scientific genius, but also for his deep commitment to the country’s development and the empowerment of its youth. His vision of a "developed India", along with his teachings and inspiring life story, continue to motivate countless individuals across the world.
